The problem in one picture

In 2025 the four-seam rode with arm-side life. In 2026 the release dropped on every pitch and the fastball turned into a cutter. Three grip fixes during the season did not bring it back. The result: every pitch now breaks toward first base, away from righties and in on lefties. Nothing moves away from a lefty.

Grips, drawn on the ball

Now · the one that worked
Deep-handed split (SPL4)

Index and middle split either side of the horseshoe. More hand on top of the ball than a classic split: ring and pinky come up alongside the fork instead of parking off to the side, closer to a split-change hold. Let it leave off the inside of the fingers, not the tips.

Why it fits him: he does not have to pronate or turn anything over. Fastball arm speed, and the hold kills the spin.

Backup · only if the hold stops repeating
Classic split, wider fork

Fingers spread wider outside the seams, more toward the tips, hand off to the side. It is the version most pitchers learn first, so it is the fallback, not the plan.

Trade: usually a touch firmer and a touch less run. If we go here, the band stays the same and the pitch has to earn it again from zero.

Cue · from the pen scriptFastball arm speed. Let it leave off the inside of the fingers. Same tunnel as the heater, then it falls. Don't guide it.

Pitch 2 · Sinker

Not measuredPath B only · decided at Gate A, end of Week 12

Joey has had a sinker as an arm-side option, but there is no measured shape for it in anything we hold. It is on this page because of what happens if the fastball does not come back. Path B stops chasing the ride, turns the hard cut into a real cutter, and gives the arm side to a one-seam sinker. The seam makes the run, so he does not have to turn his wrist over against his natural supination.

If Gate A picks Path A, this section stays parked. Nothing is lost, and nothing was thrown off a mound for it.

Grips, drawn on the ball

Target · one-seam
One-seam sinker, index ahead (SI1)

Index and middle straddle a single seam, index set slightly ahead of the middle, thumb underneath on the lower seam. One seam trips the airflow on one side of the ball in flight, and that is where the run and sink come from.

Why it fits him: it suits a supinator and the lower 2026 slot helps it. He throws a fastball and the ball does the rest.

Backup · if the first seam won't sit in his hand
One-seam sinker, fingers together (SI2)

Fingers together with the pads on one seam near the top of the ball, thumb under, held fairly deep. Same idea, a different place to put the seam. Built for a supinator with a big breaking ball who wants a diving fastball without changing how he releases.

Watch: both one-seamers are sensitive to orientation. If the seam rolls a few degrees it cuts instead of runs, which is exactly the 2026 fastball problem showing up again.

Cue · from the pen scriptSet the seam and throw a fastball. The seam makes it run, not the wrist.

Checkpoints

  1. Wk 1–3Grip cards in hand, no throwing. Both one-seam holds alongside the cutter hold, so none of Path B is new on the mound if it is needed.
  2. Gate AAfter Pen 4 (end of Week 12). If neither fastball grip reaches its band on 2 of 3 throws across Pens 3 and 4, Path B is on the table. Joey, Derek, Brian and the White Sox agree before the switch.
  3. Pen 5First sinkers off the mound (Week 13, Day 1), down and in to righties. Sets the velocity band.
  4. Pen 6Pairs. Cutter sets the line up, sinker runs in on the hands off the same start.
  5. Pen 7–8Counts. Sinker in to righties to get ahead, sinker away to lefties. HB on the pen sheet after every pitch.
What would park it: two pens of sinkers that run under +10" or that cut, or a velocity bleed that puts it in changeup range. Then the backup seam gets the same two-pen look before anything else changes.
