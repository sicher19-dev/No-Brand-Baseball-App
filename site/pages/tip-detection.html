Tip DetectionReel 01

Find the tell before a hitter does.

Tip Detection checks video of a pitcher for anything in his delivery that gives the pitch away before release. If something is there, the report shows what it is, when it shows up and how much it gives away.

How it worksReel 02

A computer watches only his body.

We track the pitcher through every delivery: head, shoulders, elbows, hands, glove, hips, knees and feet. Nothing else in the picture is used.

From that tracking alone, the computer tries to name the pitch before the ball is released. It is tested on games it has never seen. If it can name the pitch more often than a good guess would, something in the delivery is giving the pitch away.

We run this separately for each camera angle, and separately for the windup and the stretch, because a tell often shows in only one of them.

The verdictReel 03

Three possible answers.

Every camera angle and delivery gets one of three verdicts

Clear tell
A hitter who knows what to look for could use it.
Weak tell
A small but real edge.
No tell
Nothing readable before release.

Every number comes with its baseline.

A pitcher who throws his fastball half the time can be "read" half the time by anyone. So the report always shows how often the video named the pitch next to two baselines:

  • By guessing his usual pitch
  • From the count and runners alone

A tell only counts when the video beats both.

The reportReel 04

What comes back.

The report is written in plain words, for a pitcher and his coach to read together.

See the sample report

In every report

  • A verdict for each camera angle and delivery
  • The moment in the delivery the tell shows
  • The body cue, such as where the glove sits
  • A fix to work on
  • A page for each game, with clips of the flagged pitches next to a typical pitch of another type

Game only and pen confirmed

In a game, pitch choice follows the count and the hitter, so a pattern can come from the situation and not from the body. A tell found in game video alone is marked game only.

When the same tell shows up in a bullpen where the pitch types were shuffled, it is marked pen confirmed. That means it comes from the delivery.

What to sendReel 05

One clip per pitch and a pitch list.

A job is one pitcher, one season or set of pens, and one or two camera angles.

Video

One video file for each pitch, in mp4.

Pitch list

One spreadsheet file with a row for each pitch:

  • Pitch type
  • Runners on base
  • Date
  • Pitcher name

How much

We need at least 150 pitches in each pitch group (fastball, breaking and offspeed) from each camera angle. With less than that, the report says "not enough yet" and suggests a bullpen with the pitch types shuffled.

What we keep

Raw clips are kept only until the job is done. We keep the report, the still frames and the clips of the flagged pitches.

Camera guideReel 06

Two angles. One steady camera each.

Angle 01

Behind the plate

The hitter's view. This is the closest thing to what the batter sees, so it is the one to send if you can send only one.

Angle 02

Center field

The view from behind the pitcher. It shows what a runner on second base can see.

The clip works when

  • The camera is steady, on a tripod or a fixed mount
  • The whole body is in the picture, head to feet
  • It runs from the set through release without a cut

We have to reject a clip when

  • The pitcher is too small in the picture
  • The camera moves or zooms during the delivery
  • The shot cuts to a close-up in the middle of the pitch
